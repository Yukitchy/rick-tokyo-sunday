Tokyo · Sept 20 and Oct 6

Your two open days in Tokyo.

Twenty nights, and two days with nothing written on them: Sunday Sept 20 at the start, Tuesday Oct 6 near the end. Each course below is built around something from your interests list that is not booked anywhere else — and sized for how tired you are likely to be that day.

Course A · Market, garden, matcha

The market at dawn, tea by noon

On day three the clock still has you awake at five, so we use it instead of fighting it. A fish market is on your interest list and so is a tea ceremony, and both sit within two kilometres of your hotel. You are back by early afternoon with the evening free to pack for Hakone.

The day

  1. 7:30
    Leave the hotelTen minutes by taxi. You will most likely be awake anyway.
  2. 7:45
    Tsukiji Outer MarketFour blocks, around four hundred shops, at the hour the cooks are still buying. Tamagoyaki straight off the grill, scallops on the shell, dried bonito shaved in front of you. We eat as we walk.
  3. 9:30
    Breakfast in the marketA sit-down bowl once the walking is done.
  4. 10:30
    Hama-rikyu GardensTen minutes on foot. A 400-year-old shogun’s garden with saltwater ponds, sitting inside the modern skyline. Flat paths, benches all the way round.
  5. 11:00
    Matcha at Nakajima no OchayaA tea house standing out over the pond on stilts. Matcha whisked in front of you with a seasonal sweet, sitting on tatami with the water on three sides. The closest thing to a tea ceremony that needs no booking.
  6. 12:30
    Back at the hotelA twenty-minute walk or a short taxi. The afternoon is yours.

The route

Hotel → Tsukiji 10 min by taxi. Market on foot. Market → garden 10 min on foot. Garden → hotel about 20 min on foot. Open the route in Google Maps ↗

Course B · Two museums, ten minutes apart

Edo Tokyo and Hokusai

Your interest list says &ldquo;Edo Tokyo museum / Hokusai museum&rdquo;. They are in the same neighbourhood, a ten-minute walk apart, and Sunday is the day both are open. Ryogoku is also the sumo district, which makes it a useful warm-up for Osaka on the 28th.

The day

  1. 10:00
    Leave the hotelAbout 25 minutes by train. A late start after the baseball.
  2. 10:30
    Edo-Tokyo MuseumYou enter by walking across a full-size replica of the old Nihonbashi bridge, and the city unfolds underneath: a whole Edo street, a tenement house you can step into, then the earthquake, the war and the neon years. Two hours, all indoors, benches and lifts throughout. 800 yen.
  3. 12:30
    Chanko lunchThe stew sumo wrestlers eat to build weight, served in the streets where they train.
  4. 14:00
    Sumida Hokusai MuseumTen minutes on foot. Hokusai was born here and moved house ninety-three times without leaving the area. The Great Wave, the views of Fuji, and the sketchbooks where you can watch the brush thinking. This is the ink painting on your list, at its source.
  5. 15:00
    Kokugikan and Kyu-Yasuda GardenThe national sumo arena is across the street, with the handprints of past champions outside. The garden beside it is free, small and almost empty.
  6. 15:45
    Back at the hotelAbout 25 minutes by train, in time to pack for Hakone.

The route

Hotel → Ryogoku about 25 min by train. Museum → lunch 5 min on foot. Lunch → Hokusai museum 10 min. Hokusai → arena and garden 8 min. Ryogoku → hotel about 25 min. Open the route in Google Maps ↗

Keep in mind Both museums usually close on Mondays, so the 20th is the day this works. Tickets are sold at the door. Nothing here needs booking except the chanko table.

Course C · Daylight, brass bands, rockabilly

Meiji shrine and a Sunday in the park

Music and dance are on your list and nothing else on the trip covers them. Sunday is the one day Yoyogi Park fills with people playing, and the shrine forest next door is the flattest good walk in Tokyo.

The day

  1. 9:30
    Leave the hotelAbout 20 minutes by train to Harajuku.
  2. 9:50
    Meiji JinguA wide gravel path through a man-made forest of 100,000 donated trees, benches the whole way. On a Sunday morning you often catch a wedding procession crossing the courtyard under a red umbrella.
  3. 11:00
    Yoyogi ParkThe park next door is where Tokyo goes to practise in public on Sundays: brass bands, drum circles, and the rockabilly dancers who have been meeting at the entrance since the 1980s.
  4. 12:00
    Lunch on OmotesandoTen minutes on foot, away from the crowd.
  5. 13:30
    ShibuyaThe scramble crossing from above, then the quiet back streets. We stop when you have had enough.
  6. 15:30
    Back at the hotelAbout 20 minutes by train, in time to pack for Hakone.

The route

Hotel → Harajuku about 20 min by train. Shrine walk 10 min each way. Park next door. Park → Omotesando 10 min on foot. Omotesando → Shibuya 12 min on foot. Shibuya → hotel about 20 min. Open the route in Google Maps ↗

Course D · Kappabashi and Asakusa

Knives, and something to take home

Knife sharpening is on your list, and this is the day to do it. Kappabashi&rsquo;s knife shops close on Sundays, so Sept 20 was never going to work &mdash; and buying a blade at the start would mean carrying it through Hakone, Takayama, Kanazawa, Miyajima, Osaka and Kyoto. Two days before you fly, it goes straight into the suitcase.

The day

  1. 14:00
    Leave the hotelAbout 35 minutes from Shinjuku. A slow start after Disney.
  2. 14:30
    Kappabashi kitchen townEight hundred metres of nothing but kitchen equipment, where Tokyo’s restaurants buy their tools. We go to the knife shops that sharpen in the window, you hold half a dozen until one fits your hand, and they engrave your name in the blade while we walk on.
  3. 16:00
    Senso-ji and NakamiseTen minutes on foot. Tokyo’s oldest temple, the big red lantern, and the old shopping street in front of it — the easiest place in the city to finish the souvenir list.
  4. 17:30
    DinnerSee the three picks below.
  5. 19:00
    Back at the hotelAbout 35 minutes, with the knife wrapped and boxed for the flight.

The route

Hotel → Tawaramachi about 35 min by train. Kappabashi on foot. Kappabashi → Senso-ji 10 min on foot. Asakusa → hotel about 35 min. Open the route in Google Maps ↗

Course E · The rest day, with a view

Shinjuku, without going anywhere

Eighteen days in, the day after twelve hours at DisneySea, and the day before the opera and dinner in Odaiba. This is the version that asks almost nothing of you: no train, no ticket, and you can turn back to the hotel at any point.

The day

  1. 10:30
    Walk to the Metropolitan Government BuildingTen minutes from the hotel lobby, mostly under cover.
  2. 10:45
    The observatory, 202 metres upFree, no ticket, a lift straight to the top. On a clear October morning Fuji is on the west side of the floor, and the whole route you have just travelled is somewhere in that view.
  3. 12:00
    Lunch at TsunahachiTempura fried in front of you at the counter, in a Shinjuku shop that opened in 1924.
  4. 13:30
    Shinjuku GyoenA 58-hectare garden with three gardens inside it: a Japanese pond garden, a French formal one and an English lawn. Benches everywhere. We sit as much as we walk.
  5. 15:30
    Back at the hotelOn foot, in time for a nap before dinner.

The route

All of it on foot from the hotel. Observatory 10 min. Observatory → Tsunahachi 12 min. Tsunahachi → Gyoen 10 min. Gyoen → hotel 15 min, or a five-minute taxi. Open the route in Google Maps ↗

Sept 20: course A is a half day. B and C are full days.

The fee covers my time only, exactly as it did with Yuri-san: entry tickets, trains, taxis and meals are settled on the day. Nothing is owed if you wake up and decide you would rather not.

And one honest word about Oct 6.

It lands the day after twelve hours at DisneySea, and the day before the opera and dinner with Greg and Ann in Odaiba. If you get to that morning and the answer is “we are staying in”, that is a good answer, and I would rather you spend the day that way than push through it.

That is why both Oct 6 courses are half days that start late, need no tickets, and can be called off at breakfast. The knives are the one thing I would argue for — Kappabashi is shut on Sundays, so the 6th is the only day it is open while you are in Tokyo.
